Local produce

Flowers, farm goods, and sunflower products

Growing vegetables and cut flowers is part of Shilan’s agricultural story. The ATI-CAR learning-site directory lists Garden Nén Ines in Tebteb, KM 12, for ornamental plants. An ATI-CAR feature says the family garden began in the 1980s and now specializes in anthuriums, while DarJane’s focuses on ornamental plants and sunflower seed production.

A Philippine Information Agency feature describes how DarJane’s sunflower farm grew from a viewing garden into a place for developing local products. After training with the Department of Trade and Industry, the family introduced sunflower cake, sunflower butter, and sunflower-infused coffee. The article also describes coffee and flowers among the products showcased by the wider tourism village. Confirm current availability directly with each farm.

Use the social page for recent farm updates, and ask the owners about products, prices, schedules, and visitor access before making a trip.

Handmade goods and art

Meet local artists through Ayonna Mini-Gallery

Arts and crafts are part of the village’s wider product showcase, alongside coffee and flowers, according to the Philippine Information Agency. A visitor feature describes Ayonna Mini-Gallery as a place that promotes Cordilleran art and shows work by local and Benguet artists. The gallery gives visitors a way to discover handmade and visual arts connected with the area.

Browse Ayonna’s social page for recent posts and examples of its current displays. Artists, stock, and purchasing arrangements can change, so ask the gallery directly before travelling or planning a purchase.

Community projects

Tourism planned with residents

Shilan’s tourism village grew through community consultations and the formation of the Shilan Tour Guides Association, according to the Philippine Information Agency. The village connects farms, nature spots, artists, and local sellers so that visits can create opportunities for community members as well as individual attractions.

A December 2025 municipal announcement named Ayonna Mini-Gallery, Garden Nén Ines, DarJane’s Garden, Kiyomie’s Garden, and Martin’s Hobbit House among the DURE Farm Tourism Development Project’s participating attractions. It describes planned support for product development, promotion, and local capacity building. A 2025 field report also records project visits to a coffee farm, an organic farm, and local beekeepers, with discussions involving community members and the tour guides’ association.

In a March 2026 feature, the PIA reported more than 200 beneficiaries across guiding, vehicle/garage assistance, and community product sales, and noted Shilan’s second-place finish in the Department of Tourism’s 2024 Search for Best Tourism Village. These are reported milestones; check linked sources for the full context.
