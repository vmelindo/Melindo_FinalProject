Rooted in Benguet

Local character,
shared with care.

Shilan is a barangay in La Trinidad, Benguet, with a long history of exchange between the highlands and the lowlands. The municipal barangay profile traces the name to “Shalan,” described as “the way to and from,” and recounts that traders used the area as a resting place on routes linking northern Benguet with Naguilian and Pangasinan.

The same profile describes a changing livelihood: rice cultivation gave way to vegetable gardening, and commercial flower growing expanded from the mid-1950s. Gladiola, chrysanthemum, aster, and anthurium are part of that recorded agricultural story.

Today, Shilan’s barangay vision combines flowers and vegetables with commerce and eco-tourism. Community tourism builds on gardens, farms, local art, and resident-led guiding. This student guide summarizes published sources; confirm current details with the community.

Farms, culture, and shared work

Shilan’s tourism story is being shaped by residents and local partners.

Products grown and made here

A 2026 Philippine Information Agency feature describes DarJane’s Sunflower Farm developing sunflower cake, sunflower butter, and sunflower-infused coffee after business training. It also reports that coffee, flowers, arts, and crafts are showcased by the wider Shilan Community Tourism Village.

Tourism with community participation

The PIA reports that community consultations led to the Shilan Tour Guides Association. The village’s farm and garden attractions create ways for guides, farmers, and small businesses to earn from visitors. Shilan received second place in the Department of Tourism’s 2024 Search for Best Tourism Village.
